If I wanted to win two more games next season, I'd focus on defense and possession, not offense.

Here's why. Splitting this season's nine losses into close losses - three goals or fewer - versus blowouts, the close losses don't point to a scoring problem. Shooting percentage in those games was basically normal for the season. The real gap shows up somewhere else: team draw controls averaged 14.4 in wins versus 10.7 in losses, and caused turnovers averaged 9.3 in wins versus 6.7 in losses. Winning teams controlled the ball more and took it away more. That's a possession story, not a shooting story.

Given that, the one player I'd build the offseason around is Meghan Rode. She posted 75 draw controls this season - more than double anyone else on the roster - despite only playing 17 of 19 games and starting just 9. If she's on the field more consistently, that possession gap in close games likely tightens.

One honest caveat: the game log only has team-level stats, not a per-player box score for each game, so I can't prove Rode was on the bench specifically during the losses we're worried about. And looking closer at the close losses individually, the picture isn't perfectly clean - the Clemson loss, a one-goal game, had only four team draw controls, which fits the story well. But two other close losses, against Johns Hopkins and Virginia Tech, actually had sixteen draw controls each - well above what "losing" typically looked like this season. Those two games complicate the simple version of this argument.

So the recommendation stands, but with a caveat: possession numbers predict wins better than shooting numbers do, and Meghan Rode is the clearest lever for improving them. Just don't mistake one flattering example for the whole picture.
